# Demo 4 — video script

**Target: 5:00. Hard limits 3:00–6:00.**

**Format is mandatory:** headset view **plus** live-action inset of you
moving, picture-in-picture throughout. The inset matters more here than
in any other demo — the grader needs to see that you are standing still
while the world moves, which is the entire comfort argument.

The spine is **the same stretch of the world, traversed twice, once with
each locomotion system.** Choose that stretch before you record: it
should include a corner, a long run, and a vertical or awkward bit.

---

## Beat sheet

### 0:00 – 0:20 · Cold open
**On screen:** moving through the world, HUD visible with 0/5 found.
**Say:** what the space is, how many targets, how many distractors.

### 0:20 – 1:00 · The world and the search task
**On screen:** stand at a junction and look around.

**Say:**
- the layout: how many regions, and what makes them distinguishable
- **name your landmarks** and show one being used to orient
- show a target **and** a distractor side by side, and say what
  distinguishes them — this is the evidence that it is an inspection
  task and not a colour hunt
- point at the progress display

### 1:00 – 2:30 · Locomotion, both systems, same route
Traverse the chosen stretch **twice**.

**Run 1, system A.** Say its name, how it is triggered, and its
limitations — range, cooldown, what it cannot do.
**Run 2, system B, identical route.** Same.

Then the justification the rubric asks for: **which one suits this world,
and why.** Be specific to the layout. "Teleport is better here because
the corridors are short and every stop is a decision point; smooth is
better in the open hall because the distances make teleport chaining
tedious."

Switch between them **in-app**, on camera, to prove it is switchable at
runtime and not a build flag.

### 2:30 – 3:40 · Comfort, explained as sensory mismatch
Open the menu on camera and go through it item by item. This section is
where the marks are, and it is the one most people fumble by listing
features instead of explaining them.

**Say the mechanism first, once:** discomfort in VR locomotion comes from
a conflict between the visual system reporting self-motion and the
vestibular system reporting none. Every control in this menu is an
attempt to reduce that conflict.

Then, for each:
- **Vignette** — narrows the field of view during smooth motion, because
  peripheral optic flow is what drives the illusion of self-motion most
  strongly. Toggle it off and move, then on and move.
- **Snap turning** — replaces continuous rotational optic flow (the worst
  offender) with discrete jumps the brain reads as a cut, not a turn.
  Show snap, then show smooth turn, and say why you offer both.
- **Teleport fade** — removes the translation entirely; there is no optic
  flow to conflict with.
- **Adjustable speed** — lower speed, less flow.
- **View reset** — recentres the origin, for drift and for seated play.
- *(if built)* seated mode, height calibration.

### 3:40 – 4:10 · Finding the last targets
Finish the hunt on camera, or at least find one target live. Show the
completion state and the time.

### 4:10 – 4:30 · (Graduate students only) the study
One short paragraph: what you logged, how many participants, the one
change the data suggested.

### 4:30 – 5:00 · What you verified, what is broken
- **Verified:** both systems complete the hunt independently; every
  comfort toggle does something visible; settings survive a session.
- **Broken:** collision gaps, a distractor that is too obvious, a
  locomotion mode you found uncomfortable yourself and why, anything that
  does not persist across a restart.
